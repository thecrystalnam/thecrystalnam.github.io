About me

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Korean Linguistics in the Department of East Asian Languages and Literatures at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa. I advanced to candidacy in May 2026. My dissertation, When AI Misunderstands: Repair and Trouble Management in Human-AI Cooking, uses conversation analysis to study how Korean-speaking users recognize and handle trouble while cooking with a voice-based AI assistant, and how they coordinate talk with gaze, body movement, and hands-on work.

I am also an ACTFL-certified OPI tester in Korean and a Korean language instructor. From 2022 to 2025 I was instructor of record for ten Korean courses from elementary to advanced levels, including two media-analysis courses with original curricula. In my teaching I integrated AI chatbots I built myself: Sayromi, which gave elementary and intermediate students individualized speaking practice and which I evaluated in a published study, and Toguly, a custom GPT that served as a weekly debate partner for advanced learners.

My broader interests include interactional linguistics, pragmatics, multimodality, user-centered design for conversational AI, and pedagogical practices that use AI tools for language learning.

I expect to complete my Ph.D. in May 2027. I am applying for lecturer and faculty positions beginning September 2027, and I am also open to industry roles in conversational AI, AI adoption and enablement, and language education.
